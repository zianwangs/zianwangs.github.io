Distribution of entry-month CAGR

Each bar is the share of entry months with that CAGR. The black curve is the normal distribution with the same mean μ and standard deviation σ, and the dashed line marks μ. All stocks share one scale so the shapes are comparable.

Entry is the close of the chosen month, exit is the latest price. CAGR = (latest ÷ entry)1 ÷ years held − 1. “With dividends” uses Yahoo’s adjusted close.

μ and σ are the mean and standard deviation of one stock’s entry-month CAGRs. Entry months overlap and every window ends today, so these are not independent samples. Read μ and σ as a description of how buying at different times would have worked out, not as a precise estimate of a true distribution.

Only today’s constituents are included, so companies that were removed or went bankrupt are missing. Long holds look better here than they did in real time. Prices are Yahoo Finance monthly bars; the stock list comes from the datasets/s-and-p-500-companies repository, with Wikipedia as backup.
